Portland State University & the Academic Programs Paradigm Shift

A 13-year enterprise transformation — and the taxonomy system that, for the first time in the university's history, gave every academic program one consistent, centrally managed, brand-forward presence.
Director, Web Communications · Portland State University (2008–2021)
Drupal 8 platform launched Aug 25, 2020 · Acquia Cloud + Site Factory + Content Hub · Acquia Engage Awards finalist
← back to kristinboden.com

Over thirteen years I led the transformation of Portland State University's entire web presence — from a sprawl of home-grown, acronym-named sites into a governed, accessible, enterprise Drupal platform that non-technical staff across the university could run themselves. The capstone was a genuine first for PSU: a single template-driven taxonomy system that unified all 300+ academic programs under one consistent, student-first framework. The site launched with zero downtime, and it has stood essentially unchanged since the day I left — a fact I take as the truest test of how it was designed, secured, and taught.

The challenge

The PSU web had grown into roughly 340 multisites and more than 40,000 pages, much of it on aging Drupal 7, with 20+ second load times, inconsistent branding, cryptic acronym-based URLs, and no central path to accessibility (ADA / WCAG / Section 508) compliance. Central IT had no ability to police it, and every rogue departmental site made the next one look normal. The work was as much organizational as technical: unify the platform, earn adoption across teams no one directly managed, and rewrite everything into a single student-first voice — all while migrating to Drupal 8.

The paradigm shift: one taxonomy for every academic program

Working directly with Admissions and Marketing across every undergraduate and graduate college, I drove the design of a multi-layered Academic Program template system built on Acquia Content Hub. A program's data — its description, key facts, calls to apply and tour, and dynamically generated "other programs of interest" — is authored once against a common template and syndicated automatically across 50+ academic sites with no action required from the individual site owners.

For the first time in the university's history, every one of PSU's 300+ academic programs carried a consistent set of program data, in a brand-forward format, in one student-first voice — centrally manageable, and joined by the same model to news, events, and faculty profiles.

The build — 200+ sites in under three months

The redesign used a component-based, modular design system of 30–40 Drupal Paragraphs patterns across five on-brand themes, so roughly 500 content editors could build and style pages without touching code. On that foundation, 300 site authors — all working remotely during the pandemic — rebuilt more than 200 sites essentially from scratch in under three months, then joined them into a single, coherent user experience.

Remarkably, this was carried by a business-side web communications team of just 2–3 people — no in-house developer, and at times no project manager — with implementation partner Promet Source on the Acquia platform (earlier redesign phases were delivered with partner ImageX). The launch happened in two phases with zero downtime.

Platform capabilities delivered

  • A single publishing site feeding 200+ subscriber multisites for efficiency and scale
  • API-driven migration of faculty directory data
  • Media Library and the Drupal Focal Point module (editors mark the focal point so responsive cropping never loses the subject)
  • Per-multisite YouTube, Twitter, and Instagram integration
  • Removal of every acronym-based URL in favor of friendly, student-first URLs, backed by a full 404 redirect plan
  • Mobile-responsive, ADA-compliant delivery verified through usability testing

Training & enablement — the reason it stuck

None of it would have held without enablement. I built the train-the-trainer curriculum, the materials, and the ongoing support that turned 500+ non-technical content owners into capable, on-brand authors — the same champion-network model that scaled mandated training to 1,500+ users a year. Enablement, not enforcement, is what let a famously decentralized university adopt one platform and actually keep using it. It is, almost line for line, the playbook that AI-adoption and AI-literacy roles are built around today.

Governance & the "spin up a site" process

The platform was governed by a layered publishing-permissions model inside a single, centralized location for maintenance, security updates, and compliance — so autonomy for departments never came at the cost of brand, accessibility, or security. I also built a deliberately simple, repeatable process for spinning up a brand-new subsite within that governed framework, so growth could happen without sprawl. That process was the antidote to the rogue-site proliferation I'd spent a career consolidating away.

Open source — giving the model back to the field

The PSU work wasn't kept behind the firewall. Building on years of open education advocacy (including the 2012 "Intro to OpenEDU" work), the enterprise multisite model and its code were shared with the broader Drupal and higher-education community, and I presented it at Drupal business gatherings — an early, public instance of the "adopt it first, then give it back to the field" pattern that runs from my 1994 internet work to my AI work today.
